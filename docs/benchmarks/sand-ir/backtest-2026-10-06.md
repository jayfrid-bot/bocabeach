# Sand model bench, 2026-10-06

Question: is the sand model running hot in the fall, and if so why? Run with `npx vite-node -c vitest.config.ts scripts/sand_backtest.ts`. Inputs: Open-Meteo historical forecast (soil, wind) and the satellite sunshine archive (radiation), both in this folder. Observed sunshine is used with no cloud damping on top, as the live model does for an observed hour.

## Verdict

- **No model change yet.** Every knob that fixes October makes June or September worse by about the same amount. With 17 readings and 3 of them from the fall, that is noise moving around, not a fit.
- **The "stored heat" idea is dead.** Scaling the boost by sunshine accumulated since sunrise makes the error much worse everywhere (MAE 9–16 vs 6.7), because the 2026-06-23 morning reading had a full boost after only ~750 Wh. Dry sand answers to the sun right now, within minutes.
- **A small fall bias may be real.** On clear days since September the dune readings run +6.4 (9/2), −1.4 (9/4) and +6.8 (10/6) against a June–July center near 0. Three points. If the next three or four fall visits also land +4 or more at the dunes, test a sun-elevation term (sand reflects more at a low sun) — not a stored-heat term.
- **Two of the three biggest misses are input disputes, not model faults.** 7/06 (+14.7) and 7/16 (+17.4) were measured under a solid deck (GOES 99% at the time), but the sunshine archive now says 589 and 878 W/m² for those hours. The archive's overcast-day radiation is not to be trusted for this bench. 9/4 15:10 (−26.7) is the mirror case: the archive says 352 W/m² for an hour the owner saw as mostly sunny, and the sand was still 130°F from the bright hour before.
- **Today the live pipeline under-read the sun:** it carried 645 W/m² forward from a partly cloudy hour; the satellite later recorded 812. With the true number the model would have read 135–137 at the dunes, so the real miss today is 7–9°, not 5°.
